# Public accounts vs StatCan COFOG: reconciliation review, FY2024-25

_Prepared 2026-11-21 for review. The tables are generated by `make all`; the full division-by-division tables for every jurisdiction are in [`data/3_gold/reconciliation/summary.md`](../data/3_gold/reconciliation/summary.md) and [`cofog-by-division.csv`](../data/3_gold/reconciliation/cofog-by-division.csv). This file is the hand-written analysis of them._

## What is compared

| | Public accounts (PA) | StatCan |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Each jurisdiction's FY2025-26 consolidated statements, using the **2024-25 comparative column** | Table 10-10-0024-01, reference year 2024 (= FY2024-25), **preliminary** |
| Entity | Consolidated reporting entity (government plus hospitals/health authorities, school boards, most colleges) | "Provincial and territorial governments" component (government proper); "Federal government" for Canada |
| Function | Ministry or function lines mapped to COFOG divisions (`config/mappings/<code>.csv`) | CCOFOG divisions |
| Adjustment | Amortization removed (StatCan excludes consumption of fixed capital) | — |

The extractions themselves are sound. Every extracted table adds to its printed total, every mapping covers 100% of dollars, and the mapped facts tie back to each statement's total expense (`data/3_gold/validation/public-accounts.csv`). The gaps are real differences between the two sources, not reading errors.

## Findings

### 1. Amortization is the first bridge everywhere (confirmed)

StatCan's CCOFOG excludes consumption of fixed capital (table footnote 2), and every public accounts total includes amortization. Removing it moves every jurisdiction toward StatCan. For Ontario it closes the whole gap. Without this step the totals look 3–5% apart even where they agree.

Only Ontario publishes amortization by sector, so it is the only jurisdiction where the adjustment can be made category by category. Everywhere else it comes from the Statement of Change in Net Debt as a single total, and it sits on the `_unallocated` row of the division tables. That inflates the public accounts side of every division slightly (by about 3–4% of each one).

### 2. Totals fall into three groups

- **Within 3%: ON, PE, AB, BC.** The two sources agree on total spending.
- **Public accounts 5–10% higher: NS, NB, MB, NL (and federal, see 5).** The gaps are spread across Health, Education and Economic affairs. *Lead (unverified):* the reporting perimeter. These provinces' summary statements consolidate health authorities and school boards at their full expense, including what those bodies fund from their own revenue. StatCan's provincial component counts only the government's own spending and grants. For scale, StatCan's separate health-institution component is $3.8B for NL and $5.2B for NS, so even a small own-revenue share would cover the gap.
- **StatCan 12–15% higher: SK, QC.** StatCan's provincial component is larger than the whole consolidated public accounts. See 3.

### 3. Quebec and Saskatchewan: StatCan is higher (open)

**Quebec, −$27.1B.**
- **Ruled out:** refundable tax credits. Quebec's statements already present $11.1B of refundable tax credits as expenditure (Note 4, p.110), the same way StatCan treats them.
- Quebec's "Other portfolios" ($12.4B, $18.0B before amortization) can't be assigned to a function from the statement. That explains why Justice/Public order (−$4.8B) and Culture (−$2.7B) look missing, but not the total.
- The total gap sits in General public services (−$20.3B) and Social protection (−$17.3B).
- *Leads (unverified):* social-insurance bodies that StatCan may count inside Quebec's provincial government but that the public accounts treat as trusts or separate entities, such as CNESST (workers' compensation), Quebec Parental Insurance Plan benefits and SAAQ (auto insurance). General public services could also be gross transfers or interest that the public accounts net.
- Next check: Quebec Public Accounts Volume 1 note listing the reporting entity, compared with StatCan's CGFS entity list.

**Saskatchewan, −$2.9B.**
- The gap is almost all in Social protection: StatCan $4.3B vs public accounts $1.8B.
- *Lead (unverified):* the same pattern, with Workers' Compensation Board or Crown insurance benefits counted by StatCan as social security, or crop insurance classified as income support rather than agriculture. Saskatchewan's Agriculture line ($1.76B in 2024-25) is unusually large.

### 4. Education higher in public accounts, Social protection higher in StatCan (classification, partly explained)

In the large provinces the two categories move in opposite directions:

| | Education gap | Social protection gap | Health gap |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| Ontario | +14,746 | −16,020 | +3,297 |
| British Columbia | +5,550 | −7,034 | +182 |
| Alberta | +4,652 | −3,047 | +1,654 |
| Quebec | +3,250 | −17,304 | +3,498 |
| Manitoba | +2,578 | −1,330 | +590 |

- **Long-term care (strong lead).** Ontario's Health gap (+$3,297M) almost exactly equals its Long-Term Care ministry ($3,276M). StatCan appears to classify long-term care under Social protection (COFOG 10.2 Old age), while provinces report it under Health. The same likely applies to Quebec's CHSLDs, Nova Scotia's Seniors and Long-Term Care, and Alberta's continuing care. To test it, remap long-term care lines to 10.2 and rerun.
- **Child care (lead).** ON, MB, NS and PE report child care inside Education ministries. COFOG puts it in 10.4 Family and children. Under the federal-provincial child care agreements this is several billion dollars in Ontario alone.
- **The Ontario Education gap is too large for child care alone.** Ontario shows +$14.7B. A plausible piece is that the consolidated statements include school boards' and colleges' full expense, while StatCan's provincial component carries only the grants. This is the largest open question in the provincial results.

Because provincial COFOG is published only at division level, none of these splits can be confirmed from the StatCan table. They have to be tested by remapping and seeing whether the gaps close.

### 5. Federal: the ministry mapping is the limit, not the data

- Total: +$21.5B (+4.1%). This includes $4.0B of net actuarial losses (pension remeasurement), which statistical rules don't treat as expense, plus the provision for valuation.
- The division gaps are large because Finance ($216B) is mapped wholesale to General public services. Finance holds the Canada Health Transfer, Canada Social Transfer, equalization and public debt charges.
- **StatCan's federal Health ($65.2B) ≈ Health Canada ($14.1B) + the Canada Health Transfer (~$52B).** StatCan classifies the CHT by function (Health), not as a general transfer between governments. The Social Transfer is probably split between Education and Social protection the same way.
- Fix: split Finance in `config/mappings/ca.csv` with weights, using the major-transfer amounts in Note 6(a). Do the same for Employment and Workforce Development (OAS/GIS → 10.2, EI → 10.5, Canada Child Benefit → 10.4). The federal app categories need those groups anyway.

### 6. Why StatCan can't be the source for the app (confirmed)

- Provincial COFOG is published only for the 10 divisions, so there is no hospitals vs other health split for provinces.
- It lags. FY2024-25 is the latest year and is preliminary, while the public accounts already have FY2025-26.
- Its perimeter (government proper) differs from what provinces report and taxpayers see (the consolidated entity).

It is very useful as a check. Once amortization is removed, totals within a few percent confirm the extraction. The division gaps point straight at classification choices worth documenting for app users.

## Suggested next steps

1. **Federal mapping:** split Finance and Employment and Workforce Development with weights from Note 6(a); rerun. Expect the federal Health, General public services and Social protection gaps to shrink sharply.
2. **Test the long-term care hypothesis:** map ON Long-Term Care, NS Seniors and Long-Term Care, and the LTC shares of AB, QC and MB health to 10.2; rerun and see whether Health and Social protection close.
3. **Quebec perimeter:** compare the reporting entity in Quebec's Public Accounts Volume 1 with StatCan's entity classification (CNESST, QPIP, SAAQ).
4. **Saskatchewan:** check where crop insurance and WCB benefits sit.
5. **Amortization by function:** where a province publishes expense by object by function (Alberta Schedule 3 is printed rotated; Saskatchewan Schedule 15), extract it so the amortization adjustment can be made per division.
6. **Review the low-confidence mappings** (the `confidence` column in `config/mappings/*.csv`), especially lines mapped to `_unallocated` or general public services n.e.c.

## Caveats

- StatCan's 2024 values are preliminary (status `p`) and will be revised.
- 2024-25 public accounts figures come from the 2025-26 statements' comparative column. Ontario, Manitoba and the federal government restated theirs; the others are as previously reported.
- The mappings are first-pass judgments by ministry name. Each row carries a confidence level and notes.
- StatCan's hospital, school board and university/college components are shown in `summary.md` for context only. They are not consolidated with the provincial component, so adding them double-counts provincial grants.
